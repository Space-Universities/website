Securing the Final Frontier

An Interactive Investigation into Space Cybersecurity

Mission Background 🌍

Frontier-9 is an Earth-observation satellite that supports environmental monitoring and disaster response by collecting imagery of regions affected by wildfires, flooding, and other natural hazards. The spacecraft is operated through a network of ground stations responsible for receiving telemetry and transmitting commands.

During a routine communication pass, operators detect suspicious activity within the mission environment. Although Frontier-9 appears healthy and continues to communicate normally, mission personnel are concerned that the ground station may have been compromised. At present, there is no evidence that unauthorised commands have reached the spacecraft.

You are a cybersecurity analyst supporting the Frontier-9 mission. The operations team has asked you to investigate the incident, determine what happened, assess the potential impact on mission operations, and recommend appropriate security controls.

📡 Antenna / RF Front End
RF stands for Radio Frequency. Satellites communicate using radio waves.

The Antenna / RF Front End is responsible for transmitting signals to the satellite and receiving signals from it. It includes the antenna and associated radio equipment that receives, amplifies, filters, and prepares signals for processing by the rest of the ground station.

📻 Modem / SDR
Modem is short for Modulator/Demodulator, while SDR stands for Software-Defined Radio.

A modem converts digital information into communication signals and converts received signals back into digital information. An SDR performs many radio-processing functions in software rather than specialised hardware.

In modern ground stations, SDRs often perform modem functions, which is why the terms are frequently used together.

🔭 TT&C Software
TT&C stands for Telemetry, Tracking and Command.
  • Telemetry is information sent from the satellite to Earth.
  • Tracking determines the satellite's position and movement.
  • Command refers to instructions sent from Earth to the satellite.

TT&C systems allow operators to monitor spacecraft health, determine spacecraft location, and send commands to control mission activities.

🕹️ Mission Control System (MCS)
The Mission Control System is the central software platform used to manage and coordinate satellite operations.

It collects telemetry from the spacecraft, displays mission status information, stores operational data, and provides the tools needed to plan and execute spacecraft activities. It acts as the operational hub of the ground station.

🖥️ Operator Workstation
An Operator Workstation is a computer used by mission operators to interact with the rest of the ground station.

Operators use workstations to monitor spacecraft health, review telemetry, plan activities, and send commands. Although spacecraft are often highly advanced, many cyber attacks target operator workstations because they are directly used by people and often contain access credentials.

2. The First Alert 🚨

ALERT: Suspicious command activity detected!

 
You begin gathering evidence and interview the operations team.
The operations team reports:

  • The satellite is responding normally.
  • Telemetry is being received successfully.
  • The antenna is tracking correctly.
  • Several unauthorised commands appear in the command queue (not yet sent).
  • One of these commands could place the satellite into safe mode.
The source of the commands is unknown.
🛰️ Safe Mode
Safe mode is a low-risk operating state that a spacecraft enters when a problem occurs. Non-essential activities, such as imaging or scientific observations, are stopped while critical systems remain operational.

This gives operators time to investigate the issue and recover the spacecraft safely.

Knowledge Check

1. Based on the available evidence, which explanation can be ruled out most confidently?




 
Check Answer

✅ Correct.

Hardware failure
Several findings suggest that the technical components of the ground station are functioning normally:

  • Signal quality is normal.
  • No RF interference or jamming is detected.
  • Telemetry packets are being decoded successfully.
  • The satellite is responding normally.

Together, these observations indicate that the RF Front End, Modem/SDR, and spacecraft are operating as expected. While a cybersecurity incident or operator error may still be possible, the available evidence provides the strongest basis for ruling out a hardware failure.

2. Which one of the following findings provides the strongest evidence that the incident may involve unauthorised access to the ground station?





 
Check Answer

✅ Correct.

Privileged admin account creation
The creation of an unexpected privileged account is direct evidence that access permissions within the mission environment have been modified. Unlike the phishing email, which only suggests a possible attack path, the new account indicates that someone successfully performed an administrative action within mission systems.

However, a strong argument could also be made for E, since the use of the normal TT&C interface suggests the attacker may be operating through legitimate mission software rather than exploiting the communications link directly.

In a real investigation, analysts often consider multiple pieces of evidence together before reaching a conclusion.

Knowledge Check

1. At which stage could the attack have been stopped most effectively?






 
Check Answer

There are multiple valid answers, but many cybersecurity professionals would identify either:

Credential Theft or Privilege Escalation

Preventing credential theft, for example through Multi-Factor Authentication or phishing-resistant authentication, would stop the attacker before they gained access to mission systems.

Preventing privilege escalation, for example through stricter access controls and account monitoring, would limit what the attacker could do even if they gained access.

The earlier an attack is stopped, the less opportunity an attacker has to move through the system.

2. Which stage would be most difficult for operators to detect?






 
Check Answer

✅ Correct.

Telemetry Access

Unlike unauthorised commands, accessing mission data may not cause any immediate operational impact. Unless monitoring systems or alerts are in place, data access activity can blend in with normal mission operations and remain unnoticed for long periods.

This is why confidentiality-related incidents are often harder to detect than availability-related incidents. Many organisations implement logging, monitoring, and anomaly detection systems specifically because data access activities can otherwise blend into normal operations.

3. Which stage presents the greatest risk to mission operations?






 
Check Answer

✅ Correct.

Unauthorised Commands

This stage has the most direct potential to affect spacecraft behaviour and mission objectives. Depending on the commands issued, consequences could include:

  • Entering safe mode
  • Disabling payload operations
  • Interrupting data collection
  • Consuming limited spacecraft resources
  • Causing loss of mission functionality

While earlier stages enable the attack, unauthorised command activity represents the point at which the attacker can directly influence spacecraft operations.

 

4. What was Compromised? ⚠️

You have now reconstructed the most likely attack path and its timeline.

Although the attacker did not gain direct access to Frontier-9, several security weaknesses were exploited throughout the incident.

Cybersecurity incidents can compromise different security properties:

Security Property Description
Authentication Verifying the identity of a user, system, or process
Integrity Ensuring data or systems are not modified without authorisation
Non-repudiation Providing evidence that an action occurred and who performed it
Confidentiality Preventing unauthorised access to information
Availability Ensuring systems and services remain accessible when needed
Authorisation Determining what actions an authenticated user is permitted to perform

By identifying which of these were affected at each stage of the attack, investigators can better understand the consequences of the incident and the risks posed to mission operations.

5. Defend the Mission 🛡️

The investigation is complete.

You have concluded that the incident most likely began with a phishing email, which allowed an attacker to gain access to the mission systems using stolen credentials. Although the attack was detected before any commands reached the satellite, the incident has raised concerns about the security of the mission's ground systems.

Following the investigation, the mission director has approved funding for only two cybersecurity improvements this year.

Unfortunately, budget and staffing constraints mean that not every security recommendation can be implemented immediately.

Challenge

Your task is to recommend which two improvements should be prioritised. Choose carefully.

Available Controls:

Control Description
Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) Additional verification during login
Command Approval Workflow Two operators must approve critical commands
RF Encryption Protects communications over the radio link
Security Awareness Training Reduces phishing success
Additional Antenna Improves communications redundancy
Digitally Signed Audit Logs Provides reliable evidence of who performed actions

Check Answer
🛡️ Suggested Answer: Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) + Command Approval Workflow

MFA addresses the most likely entry point into the system by making stolen credentials significantly more difficult to use.
Command Approval Workflow reduces the operational impact of a compromise by ensuring that critical spacecraft commands require approval from multiple authorised operators.

Together, they provide two complementary layers of defence: Prevent Access + Limit Damage.

This combination addresses both:

  • the likely root cause of the incident (stolen credentials)
  • the most serious operational consequence (unauthorised spacecraft commands)
🛡️ Alternative Answer: Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) + Security Awareness Training

It may be argued that phishing was the initial attack vector. This combination focuses on preventing credential compromise through both technical controls, such as MFA, and human-focused controls, such as security awareness training.

🛡️ Alternative Answer: Command Approval Workflow + Digitally Signed Audit Logs

Others who prioritise mission assurance may select controls that focus on limiting mission impact and improving accountability.
While this combination may not prevent the initial compromise, it can help reduce the consequences of unauthorised actions and improve post-incident investigation.

 

6. Incident Response 🕹️

Although the unauthorised command was intercepted, investigators cannot determine whether the attacker still has access to the Mission Control System (MCS).

The next communication pass is scheduled to begin in 15 minutes, providing the next opportunity to communicate with Frontier-9 and receive mission data. The mission team must decide how to balance spacecraft security, mission operations, and ongoing investigation of the incident.

Challenge

You can only perform two actions immediately. Which two actions would you prioritise and why?

Action
Disable the suspicious account
Shut down command transmission
Reset all operator passwords
Monitor the suspicious account
Preserve evidence for investigation
Continue normal operations

Check Answer
🕹️ Suggested Answer: Disable the suspicious account + Shut down command transmission

These actions prioritise protecting the spacecraft by limiting the attacker's ability to access mission systems or transmit additional commands.

🕹️ Alternative Answer: Disable the suspicious account + Continue normal operations

These actions attempt to balance security and mission continuity. The suspected attacker loses access, but planned mission activities can continue while the investigation proceeds.

🕹️ Alternative Answer: Monitor the suspicious account + Preserve evidence for investigation

These actions prioritise understanding the attacker's behaviour and collecting evidence before taking action. However, they also increase the risk that the attacker remains active.

🕹️ Discussion

There is no single correct answer. Each response prioritises a different objective:

Objective Example Actions
Containment Disable suspicious account + Shut down command transmission
Mission Continuity Disable suspicious account + Continue normal operations
Investigation Monitor suspicious account + Preserve evidence for investigation

🕹️ Incident response trade-offs

In practice, incident response teams often need to balance competing priorities. Prioritising one objective may reduce your ability to achieve the others.

  • Strong containment may interrupt mission activities.
  • Maintaining mission operations may allow an attacker to remain active.
  • Prolonged investigation may provide valuable intelligence but increase operational risk.

Different organisations may make different decisions depending on mission objectives, risk tolerance, available evidence, and the potential consequences of a successful attack.

7. Mission Impact Assessment 🎯

Suppose the unauthorised command had succeeded.

Then the satellite would:

  • Enter safe mode
  • Suspend payload operations
  • Stop collecting environmental monitoring imagery
  • Require operator intervention

Challenge

Which statement best explains why this incident is significant?




 

Check Answer

 

🎯 Discussion

The most important consequence of this incident is not physical damage to the spacecraft.

The satellite may continue to function normally, communicate with the ground station, and remain healthy in orbit. However, if it cannot perform its intended mission, such as collecting imagery or scientific data, or if that material is stolen, then the mission’s objectives may be compromised.

🎯 Key Concept: Spacecraft Health ≠ Mission Success

A spacecraft can survive a cyber incident while the mission itself still suffers significant consequences.

Examples include:

  • Missing time-sensitive observations
  • Losing valuable scientific data
  • Delaying operational objectives
  • Increasing recovery and operational costs
  • Reducing stakeholder confidence in the mission

For many organisations, the ultimate goal of cybersecurity is not simply to protect hardware — it is to ensure that mission objectives can still be achieved.

While this scenario is fictional, the techniques used by the attacker are based on methods that have been observed in real-world cyber incidents. A good example is the 2022 Viasat KA-SAT cyberattack, in which the ground segment was compromised and legitimate commands were executed without authorisation, resulting in service disruption for tens of thousands of customers [1]. Security analysts often use threat frameworks to describe these techniques, understand how attacks progress, and identify appropriate defensive measures.

SPARTA, which stands for Space Attack Research and Tactic Analysis, is a framework developed by The Aerospace Corporation based in the US to help organisations analyse cyber threats to space systems. It contains a catalogue of attack techniques and countermeasures that may be relevant to this scenario. Many of these techniques can affect one or more of the security properties discussed earlier in this exercise.
