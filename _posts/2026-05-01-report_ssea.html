Inspiration and Industry Context

 The 5th Symposium on Space Education Activities (SSEA) had 515 participants from 39 countries, 170 oral presentations across 4 parallel sessions, and 49 posters. It opened with a focus on inspiration and ambition in space education, using the Artemis II and Orion ESA video to set the tone. Keynotes from ESA and DLR leaders emphasized Europe’s need to remain sovereign and competitive in space, and the importance of ambitious, interdisciplinary education to address skills gaps - especially in communication, adaptability, and systems thinking. 

ESA Academy: Expanding Teaching Models

The ESA Academy is evolving its approach: 

  • Scholarships & Training: Two new initiatives (student associations, case studies) are launching soon, and online training opportunities (via their ESALearn onlinen platform) are being expanded.
    (Note: the ESA Academy Case Studies Programme has since been launched)  
  • Hands-on Learning: The next ESA academy CubeSat summer school is in 2027. The academy as a whole has increased numbers of ‘downstream’ relevant courses (e.g. Earth Observation & Technology Transfer) compared to traditional ‘upstream’ (i.e. Cubesats and Rockets). 
  • Industry-Academia Link: ESA recruits ~110 graduate trainees per year (opening in February) and ~60 research fellows per year, offering direct pathways for students. 
  • DLR has National (German) Graduate Trainee Programme with ESA, whereby 6 Masters’ graduates each year are guaranteed to start careers with ESA. Likely that this will continue to be expanded with other national agencies. 

Takeaway: UK universities should take advantage of ESA’s practical, project-based learning and training courses to boost employability. 

Teaching Strategies

Practical, Project-Based Learning (PBL)
  • ETH Zurich’s Space Systems Masters: Students undertake two 8-week PBL activities (a mission project, producing an 80 page ‘Red Book’ and a Venus probe design/build/test). 30% of students complete theses with external organisations. Only second year running programme, so can’t see industry feedback. First cohort noted that the workload needed to be better balanced.
  • Oslo Space Systems Masters: 10-15 student cohort, running since 2003. PBL in collaboration with Andøya Space for CanSat launches on weather ballons and 5-day intensive AIT training. Students report high relevance to future careers.
  • Polytechnico di Torino Space Systems Masters (Concurrent and MBSE module): 150 student cohort, split into 10-15 to perform a team feasibility assessment, based on a real workflow. From 2024, introduced 4 concurrent design sessions (MATLAB/STK/Valispace/Excel), with students suggesting Valispace be used as single source of truth. Originally, manual bridging between Valispace and Capella, developed a python bridge.
  • TUM/Stuttgart Life Support Simulation: Small groups (8 students) model life support systems using MATLAB/C, with graded homework and real-world problem-solving.
  • Stuttgart VLEO Workshop: 1-week concurrent design workshop, 38 participants from 15 countries, mission statement changes annually, uses ASTOS, Python, MATLAB.
  • ROSPIN Summer School: 9-week workshop on EO mission design, satellite data processing, and academic research; 100 accepted, 83 completed. 

Key: Staged, hands-on projects with clear industry relevance are most effective at maintaining student interest and to find students roles after graduated. Universities could consider running similar short courses in summer around areas for which they have research groups. ROSPIN seems to be like UKSEDS, maybe potential for a similar workshop.

Addressing Skills Gaps
  • Concurrent Engineering & MBSE (Turin): Students use standard to simulate real workflows, bridging theory and practice. 
  • v-HAB (Virtual Habitat): Teaches Object Oriented Programming and system modeling for life support, despite a steep learning curve. Biweekly sessions and templated exercises help scaffold learning. 
  • GSOC (German Space Operations Centre) Academy: 5-day spacecraft operations course, semester-long student courses, and consulting. Pipeline includes flight director and collision avoidance training. 

Insight: Integrated tools and frameworks help students manage complexity and mirror industry workflows 

Outreach and Early Engagement
  • Budapest’s 2Space Team: High school workshops, nanosat hackathons (Qube2Space), SpaceFest (25,000+ participants). 
  • Hungarian UniSpace Consortium: Dual-entry MSc with hands-on projects (e.g., Masat-1 picosat). 
  • OeWF (Austria): “Mars” habitat in Oman, 12-15 peer-selected experiments per mission, high school shadowing program. 
  • EU Erasmus EXPLORE: “Astronaut for a week” training, simulated CO2 crisis, students report increased maturity. 
  • ESA Rocket Training: 120 students online, 100 in-person for build/launch, diverse backgrounds, uses ESALearn for lectures/quizzes. 

Lesson: Early, practical engagement (e.g., school visits, hackathons) can bridge the gap between education and industry. 

Challenges and Solutions
  • Mid-Career Bottleneck: 41% of vacancies are mid-career, with juniors and interns making up another 44%. Upskilling funds and industry-academia collaboration are critical to ensure that skills gaps are being met and mid and early careers levels.
  • Academic Perception: Students feel programs lack practical preparation. Interdisciplinary, PBL courses address this.
  • Inspiration: 96% cite inspiration (exploration, tech, climate) as a key motivator. Embed storytelling and real-world impact in teaching.
  • Mentorship: ESA faces 40% retirement by 2032. Mentoring is most relevant at career transition steps; personalisation and regular interaction are key. 
Analogues, Analog Missions & Public Outreach
  • OeWF: Analog missions with high school shadowing, team ownership, and real stakes. 
  • MDRS (ISAE-SUPAERO): 4-week Mars analog mission, science/tech demo/outreach, 3D modeling/prototyping. 
  • Space Comms: ARISS (amateur radio on ISS), MOMSTER (portable radio meteor station for classrooms), Radiometeor Zooniverse. 

Takeaway: Analog missions and citizen science projects make space tangible and inspire public engagement. 

Actionable Recommendations
  1. Adopt PBL: Integrate 8–12 week mission projects (e.g., CanSat, life support simulations, VLEO workshops) into curricula. 
  2. Integrate short, intensive classes into modules: small micro-cohorts, hands-on, led by PhD/Post-Doc, with research/industry tools and practical deliverables (e.g., TUM’s 3-week AlgaeTech for 8 students) 
  3. Leverage Industry Tools: Use Valispace, MATLAB, STK, ASTOS, and Python in teaching to mirror real workflows. 
  4. Expand Outreach: Partner with schools for hackathons, workshops, and competitions (e.g., Qube2Space, ESA CanSat). 
  5. Bridge the Mid-Career Gap: Develop upskilling programs, industry-aligned research fellowships, and mentorship schemes. 
  6. Emphasize Interdisciplinarity: Ensure courses attract STEM-wide backgrounds, not just aerospace. 
  7. Support Analog Missions: Encourage participation in analog missions (e.g., MDRS, OeWF) and citizen science projects (e.g., MOMSTER). 
  8. Promote Lifelong Learning: Advocate for industry to invest in upskilling and lifelong learning opportunities. 

Final Thought

The most successful programs combine rigor, real-world relevance, and inspiration-balancing technical depth with broad systems thinking and public engagement.
